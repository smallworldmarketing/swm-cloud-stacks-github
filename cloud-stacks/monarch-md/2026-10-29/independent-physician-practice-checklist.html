Independent Physician Practice Checklist Before Going Concierge

A readiness checklist for an independent concierge practice, organized around responsibilities, patient communication, systems, staffing, and evidence of completion.

An independent physician practice checklist is most useful when it records evidence rather than a collection of reassuring checkmarks. Before opening or converting to a concierge arrangement, the physician should be able to show who owns each essential responsibility and how the work has been tested. The categories below provide a practical review structure for a women's health practice. They are not an exhaustive regulatory checklist or a substitute for professional advice. Adapt the list to the actual specialty scope and jurisdiction, and mark unresolved items clearly instead of assuming they will be addressed later.

Confirm the practice scope and decision owners

Check that the service description, patient population, appointment types, and outside care arrangements have been reviewed by the physician. Identify who can approve changes to each element. Keep the current version available to the people preparing patient materials and configuring systems. Record professional reviews that are still required and the person responsible for arranging them. A completed scope review should produce a usable description, not merely a statement that the practice will be patient-centered. Ask staff to explain which requests the office handles, which need clinical review, and which belong elsewhere.

Investigate inconsistent answers before opening enrollment.

Review agreements through the appropriate advisers

Create an inventory of the agreements associated with the practice and record their review status. Depending on the arrangement, these may involve facilities, employment, vendors, support services, membership terms, and payer relationships. Have qualified advisers evaluate the actual documents and identify dependencies. Avoid using a general internet checklist to decide whether a particular provision is acceptable. The operational task is to ensure that agreements are accounted for, the responsible people have reviewed them, and important commitments are reflected in the implementation plan. Store final versions in an organized location with appropriate access and a clear document owner.

Check continuity and communication arrangements

For a practice transition, identify the process for informing patients and managing ongoing responsibilities. The AMA's termination guidance emphasizes notice and appropriate assistance with transfer when a physician ends a patient relationship. Confirm that clinical decisions in this area have an accountable physician owner. Review the patient explanation of membership and the route for questions or declining participation. Test whether staff can identify the current approved materials. Record how unresolved requests are tracked and who reviews them. The checklist should distinguish a message sent from a patient need resolved; those are different kinds of evidence.

Verify systems, access, and recovery procedures

List the systems used for records, scheduling, communication, and administration. Confirm who administers each system and how access is granted or removed. HHS security resources address safeguards for electronic protected health information in covered settings; obtain expert review of applicable obligations. Test ordinary access with fictional records and verify the documented procedure for an outage. Ask how the practice retrieves information when a vendor relationship changes. Mark a system ready only after the relevant workflow has been exercised. A paid invoice or an activated account does not demonstrate that staff can use the system reliably in the intended process.

Confirm staffing and backup coverage

Check role descriptions against the actual tasks expected each day. Identify who handles the phone, membership administration, scheduling changes, external coordination, and questions needing clinical escalation. Ask each person to identify their backup and the documentation that backup would use. Include absences, turnover, and training of future staff in the review. Evaluate the physician's administrative workload explicitly instead of hiding it in unassigned tasks. A small practice can have a simple organization chart while still needing clear responsibilities. Where one role spans several functions, confirm that the proposed workload and decision boundaries are understood by everyone involved.

Close the checklist with a readiness decision

For every essential item, record an owner, evidence of completion, and any remaining limitation. Separate items that prevent launch from improvements that can be scheduled afterward. Conduct a final rehearsal of a fictional patient's administrative journey and document the defects found. Decide who can approve opening and who can pause it if a critical dependency changes. Keep the completed checklist as a reference for the first operational review rather than filing it away as a ceremonial milestone. Its value lies in showing what the team has actually confirmed and which responsibilities continue after the first day.

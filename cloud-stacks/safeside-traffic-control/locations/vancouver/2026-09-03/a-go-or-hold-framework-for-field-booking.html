A Go-or-Hold Framework for Field Booking

A Go-or-Hold Framework for Field Booking. Practical preparation and next-step questions for Vancouver, BC.

Booking should follow a readiness decision, not substitute for one. A date and purchase order can secure commercial capacity, but they cannot settle jurisdiction, authorize road occupation, define an incomplete plan, or verify the people assigned later. Before committing, a buyer should know what is being purchased, what evidence is still missing, which party owns each dependency, and what happens if the site or schedule changes. This framework groups the essential questions into four gates so a project can proceed, proceed conditionally, or pause without pretending every detail is already fixed.

Pass the scope and authority gates

At the scope gate, ask: What is the underlying activity? Where are its expected limits? Which phases, shifts, mobilizations, and work windows are included? Is the task construction, utility, maintenance, road work, or a planned event, and does its operating pattern change over time? Which movements may be affected—travel lanes, curb uses, cycling, walking, transit, properties, deliveries, or emergency access? Record confirmed facts separately from assumptions. Ask the provider to restate its deliverables. Does the engagement cover field crews, lane-closure support, event traffic control, traffic-management-plan support, or a defined combination?

Who supplies site information, conducts or contributes to risk assessment, prepares required documents, coordinates implementation, briefs personnel, monitors conditions, keeps records, and removes or restores the work area? The labels are less important than the written boundary. At the authority gate, ask who controls every occupied space and what current process applies. Who prepares and submits information? Who answers comments? Who pays external fees? Who receives the decision and distributes conditions? What is the effect of a delayed, revised, or refused application? Make clear that the authority retains its own decision-making role.

Neither a booking nor a provider statement guarantees approval. Confirm the governing references. Ask which edition of the provincial manual, regulation, authority standard, permit condition, or project document is expected to apply. The WorkSafeBC regulation and official traffic resources should be consulted directly for occupational requirements and training context. The provincial manual must be used within its proper jurisdictional and project setting. Record a verification date because online and project-controlled material can change.

Pass the delivery, change, and closeout gates

At the delivery gate, ask who will fill each role and when names become available. What qualification, training, supervision, or professional input does the role require? How will evidence be checked without collecting unnecessary personal information? BCCSA describes its Traffic Control Person program, but the applicability and current status of an individual still require suitable verification. Confirm the handoff package: controlled plan or drawing where required, authority conditions, hazard information, contact list, work schedule, access arrangements, emergency procedure, briefing method, and escalation chain. Ask how the provider confirms that field personnel hold the same current version reviewed by the project team.

At the change gate, test realistic scenarios. What if the construction work expands, the shift runs late, weather reduces visibility, an access conflict emerges, queues differ from expectations, equipment changes, or an event attendance pattern shifts? Who may pause work? Who assesses the consequence? What needs authority consultation or revised documentation? How are extra costs and schedule effects authorized? Answers should describe a process, not promise effortless flexibility. At the closeout gate, ask who confirms removal, restoration, document return, incident reporting, unresolved public concerns, and lessons for later phases. Define the records delivered, their timing, retention, and ownership.

For recurring work, decide how findings enter the next scope rather than disappearing into an inbox. Apply the final rule: proceed only when critical gates are evidenced; proceed conditionally when a named dependency has an owner, deadline, and limit on further commitment; hold when an unknown could invalidate permission, safe implementation, access, or the work method. Write the outcome into the booking documents. That record protects both buyer and provider by showing what was agreed, what remained provisional, and which event requires a new decision.

Questions this guide answers

Does booking a provider authorize a lane closure?

No. Commercial booking and authority permission are separate, and any applicable conditions still need to be incorporated into site-specific planning.

When is a conditional booking reasonable?

Only when the unresolved dependency has a named owner, deadline, consequence, and clear limit on what may proceed.

How should Safeside Traffic Control's certification statement be treated?

Attribute it to the company, request relevant current evidence for assigned personnel, and do not portray it as a blanket regulator endorsement.

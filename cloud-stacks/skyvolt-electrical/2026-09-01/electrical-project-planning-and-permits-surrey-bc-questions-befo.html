electrical project planning and permits Surrey BC: Questions Before Booking

A booking checklist for verifying contractor identity, authority assumptions, EV-charging scope, responsibilities, access, and final records.

Booking should follow a documented fit check, especially when a project may involve a panel change, EV charger, renovation, tenant improvement, or permit. This checklist helps an owner decide whether a contractor conversation has produced enough clarity to authorize the next step. That next step may be an assessment rather than installation. The questions do not verify a provider automatically, guarantee approval, or determine charger compatibility and electrical capacity. Ask candidates the same essentials, check current credentials through the appropriate live record, and obtain material answers in writing. A polished website or confident verbal estimate is not a substitute for a defined scope and accountable process.

Confirm provider fit, authority, and technical boundaries

Ask for the legal business name and the identity and role of the person who will be responsible for the work. What current licence, qualification, or authorization applies to the proposed activity? Who will perform and supervise field work? How can the status be verified through the relevant live record? Do not treat an old invoice, logo, directory listing, or customer comment as current credential evidence. Describe the property and desired outcome, then ask whether the provider handles that project type. Residential and commercial labels alone are too broad. State whether the request concerns new construction, renovation, tenant improvement, troubleshooting, panel work, wiring, lighting, outlets, switches, heating, an inspection, or EV charging. Ask about genuine limitations and any design, utility, general-contractor, landlord, strata, or other-trade coordination the owner must arrange. For permitting, first ask which authority is expected to have jurisdiction and how that answer will be confirmed. Surrey and Technical Safety BC maintain distinct electrical-permit resources. Then ask who will apply, whose name will be used, what documents are needed, who schedules inspections, how requested corrections are addressed, and what proves closure. Nobody should promise permit approval. If a homeowner-permit route is being considered, review the authority’s current eligibility and responsibility guidance rather than assuming it is available. Ask the provider to draw the technical boundary. Which conclusions require a site visit? Who completes load calculations or service assessment when required? Who confirms selected equipment is suitable? Who interprets applicable requirements? Reject pressure to accept remote certainty about a fault, panel capacity, charger compatibility, or guaranteed compliance. These questions should produce a plan for competent evaluation, not invite the owner to make technical determinations.

Use project-specific booking checks and define the handover

For EV charging, explain the vehicle or anticipated vehicles, normal parking position, typical travel and charging expectations, indoor or outdoor location, cable reach needs, connectivity preferences, and whether future vehicles matter. Ask the electrician what site information and equipment documents are required. BC Hydro recommends working with an electrician and offers questions for charger-installation discussions, but the provider must evaluate the actual property. Do not purchase equipment on an assumption that a connector, rating, panel, service, network feature, or incentive will work for the project. Rebate eligibility and availability require separate current verification. For renovation and lighting work, provide room plans, fixture models, control intent, ceiling and wall conditions, cabinet layouts, accessibility goals, exterior exposure, and other-trade contacts. Ask who supplies products, checks them before installation, protects finished surfaces, performs cutting and restoration, and resolves substitutions. For commercial premises, add operating hours, escorts, shutdown approval, security procedures, tenant communication, and critical equipment. Ask what the booking actually reserves. Is it a diagnostic assessment, planning visit, installation slot, or provisional date dependent on permits and products? What information must be supplied beforehand? Who needs to attend? What access and working area must be available? What cancellation, rescheduling, deposit, or change terms are being offered in the actual agreement? This guide supplies no claims about price, response time, availability, or duration. Establish a safety communication path. Tell the provider about observed outages, damage, heat, odour, noise, water, shock, smoke, or arcing. Do not reproduce symptoms. Active danger requires emergency action as appropriate, while heat, burning smell, shock, or water exposure should not wait inside an ordinary booking queue. Never open a panel, test live conductors, bypass protection, or keep suspect equipment operating to help a contractor quote remotely. Before authorizing work, request a written scope showing locations, quantities, inclusions, exclusions, owner-supplied items, access assumptions, permit roles, inspection arrangements, restoration, change approval, payment terms, and dependencies. Define the expected handover: invoices, approved changes, product literature, schedules, permit and inspection records, and unresolved-item documentation as applicable. Book when identity and current credentials can be checked, the appointment type is explicit, authority questions have an owner, dangerous conditions have been escalated, and technical unknowns are reserved for qualified assessment. Otherwise, request clarification or limit authorization to the investigation needed to produce reliable evidence.

Questions this guide answers

What should an EV-charging booking request include?

Provide vehicle and parking details, charging goals, proposed location, product information, cable-reach needs, connectivity preferences, access constraints, and future plans for professional assessment.

Should an owner buy a charger before speaking with an electrician?

Avoid assuming suitability. Discuss the site, goals, equipment, capacity assessment, authority process, and any current program conditions before committing.

What must be clear before installation is authorized?

Confirm scope, appointment type, credentials, access, products, authority roles, inspection responsibility, changes, restoration, payment terms, dependencies, and expected records.

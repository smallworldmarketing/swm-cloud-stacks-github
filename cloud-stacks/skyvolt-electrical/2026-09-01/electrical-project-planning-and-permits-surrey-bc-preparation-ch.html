electrical project planning and permits Surrey BC: Preparation Checklist

A decision framework for assembling room plans, fixture schedules, access details, approvals, and authority questions before electrical work.

Preparation should reduce ambiguity without pushing an owner into technical design. This decision framework produces a compact project packet for a contractor, designer, landlord, or authority conversation. It is particularly useful for outlet and switch installations tied to kitchens, offices, accessibility changes, furniture plans, or tenant improvements. The central choice is whether the project can proceed to proposal, needs design coordination first, or requires an investigative visit. Keep measurements and preferences separate from electrical conclusions. Never remove plates or covers, open a panel, test live wiring, or expose conductors to gather information. Safe preparation is documentary and observational.

Assemble the location and function packet

Create one sheet for each affected room or commercial zone. Give the space a stable name that everyone can use on drawings, photographs, quotations, and change records. Add a simple plan showing doors, windows, fixed cabinets, counters, desks, displays, beds, televisions, appliances, mechanical equipment, and intended parking where relevant. Indicate dimensions as field references, not construction-ready design unless they were prepared and verified by the responsible professional. Build an outlet and switch schedule based on function. For each requested point, state what the user wants to operate, its preferred location, mounting or accessibility considerations, and any furniture or equipment conflict. Identify whether the request is new installation, relocation, replacement, or repair. Avoid specifying wiring methods, circuit arrangements, protection, device ratings, or box types. Those choices depend on the installation and applicable requirements. For switches, describe the user experience: which lights or equipment should be controlled, from which entrances or work positions, and whether dimming, timers, sensors, or smart controls are being considered. Record the exact proposed product if one has already been selected, but mark it as subject to compatibility review. For outdoor locations, document exposure, mounting surface, desired operating pattern, and nearby landscaping without assuming weather or electrical suitability. Add a fixture and equipment schedule. Include manufacturer, model, quantity, dimensions, mounting information, control needs supplied by the manufacturer, and delivery status. Flag owner-supplied products. Ceiling fans, specialty lights, heaters, kitchen equipment, signage, and controls can involve structural, mechanical, or product-specific questions beyond ordinary placement. Ask who is responsible for each review. Photograph rooms from wide angles and give each image a filename linked to the schedule. Only photograph safely accessible exterior conditions. A close image without context can mislead, so pair it with a room view and written location. Do not photograph by entering restricted electrical rooms without authorization or approaching damaged equipment.

Run three gates before requesting installation

Gate one is project authority and permission. Confirm the property owner, tenant, landlord, strata, or manager who can approve the work. Determine whether drawings or design review are required under the lease, construction contract, or building process. Identify the electrical permitting authority for the site by checking current Surrey and Technical Safety BC information rather than assuming jurisdiction. Ask the applicable authority or qualified contractor about permit requirements; do not decide them from this checklist. Gate two is readiness. Are cabinetry, furniture layouts, equipment models, and reflected ceiling decisions stable enough to place devices? Are wall and ceiling finishes staying, opening, or being rebuilt? Is asbestos or other hazardous-material information required before disturbance? Are access dates, keys, escorts, loading arrangements, parking, and work-hour restrictions known? Has another trade identified its electrical requirements in writing? If critical inputs are unsettled, request design coordination instead of an installation date. Gate three is safety and investigation. Separate desired changes from observed symptoms. Write the date, location, operating condition, and repeat pattern of any outage, flicker, unusual sound, tripping, damage, or intermittent device. Do not repeatedly reset protection or keep operating suspect equipment to reproduce a symptom. Heat, smoke, arcing, burning odour, shock, or water exposure calls for immediate safe escalation, not routine preparation. Keep people away and seek appropriate emergency or qualified help according to the circumstances. Choose one of three routes after the gates. Proceed to comparable proposals when functions, locations, product responsibilities, permissions, access, and timing are stable. Schedule an assessment when existing conditions or symptoms must be examined before a scope can be responsibly proposed. Pause for design coordination when cabinetry, equipment, ceilings, tenant layouts, or other systems remain in motion. Issue the same packet to each candidate and request marked-up questions. Preserve versions with dates, because a moved cabinet or substituted fixture can invalidate earlier assumptions. Before authorization, merge accepted revisions into a final scope and identify who owns permit submission, inspection arrangements, restoration, product procurement, and closeout records. Preparation is complete when uncertainty is visible and assigned, not when every technical question has been answered by the owner.

Questions this guide answers

Should an owner specify circuit or wiring details in the preparation packet?

No. Describe functions, locations, products, constraints, and preferences. Leave circuit design, protection, wiring methods, and code application to qualified professionals.

What if cabinetry or equipment selections are still changing?

Pause installation booking and coordinate the design inputs first. Unstable dimensions or models can make placement assumptions obsolete.

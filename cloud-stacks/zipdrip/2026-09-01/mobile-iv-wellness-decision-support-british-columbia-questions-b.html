mobile IV wellness decision support British Columbia: Questions Before Booking

A pre-booking checklist covering provider registration, clinical authority, ingredients, consent, mobile privacy, records, and emergency escalation.

A booking form can collect contact and payment information without answering the questions that make an elective mobile IV decision informed. Before reserving a Vancouver or Lower Mainland visit, obtain written details about professional responsibility, assessment, products, consent, the setting, documentation, and response planning. A refusal to answer is information in itself, though it does not establish wrongdoing. This checklist is independent education, not medical advice, diagnosis, or a recommendation to book, cancel, choose an ingredient, or accept a dose. Reviewed September 1, 2026; verify the regulator pages, federal product records, and clinical wording again before publication and before every monthly reuse.

Questions about people, authority, and proposed substances

Who will assess me, and what is that person’s full name and professional designation? Who will authorize or determine the plan? Who will administer it, monitor me, and respond afterward? May I verify each person’s current registration before paying? If different people perform these functions, how are information and responsibility transferred? What professional basis permits the proposed activity in these circumstances? Is there an order, and if so, who is responsible for it? Is the professional relying on autonomous scope? How do current medication standards apply? BCCNM maintains distinct materials on medication practice, acting with orders, and autonomous scope. A business representative should not reduce these questions to “a nurse will come.” The involved professional should explain their accountable role. What assessment occurs before a package is selected? Will it address current symptoms, health history, prescriptions, non-prescription medicines, natural health products, allergies, interactions, prior reactions, recent intake, and relevant conditions? What information could lead to delay, refusal, referral, or a different plan? Can assessment happen privately if an employer, event organizer, friend, or family member arranged the booking? What exactly might be administered? Request every proper name, brand when applicable, strength, dosage form, proposed quantity, route, manufacturer, and claimed DIN or NPN. Ask whether substitutions or optional additions are possible and how those trigger reassessment. Search a DIN through Health Canada’s Drug Product Database and an NPN in the Licensed Natural Health Products Database. Match the product precisely. A database result does not prove approval of an IV route, combined mixture, advertised benefit, quantity, or use for an individual. What claims are being made? Ask for the evidence and limitations behind language about hydration, recovery, hangovers, immunity, energy, NAD+, performance, weight loss, detoxification, anti-aging, post-viral symptoms, or disease. Do not accept a testimonial, influencer post, or ingredient description as a substitute for product-specific and person-specific clinical reasoning.

Questions about consent, location, records, and problems

When and how will informed consent occur? Will the professional explain the proposed intervention, important risks, uncertainties, alternatives including doing nothing, privacy implications, costs, and the right to refuse or withdraw? What happens to payment if assessment supports not proceeding? Does any plan change require a new discussion? BCCNM’s consent material is a useful official reference for asking whether consent is informed and voluntary. How is the mobile space evaluated? Ask about lighting, stable seating, clean surfaces, hand hygiene, privacy, product transport and storage, identity checks, interruptions, children, pets, secure supplies, sharps disposal, and access for emergency responders. For offices, hotels, weddings, parties, or corporate events, ask how individual assessment and records remain separate from organizers and other participants. What observations are taken before and during administration? Which findings cause the professional to pause, stop, refer, or call emergency services? What equipment, training, communication method, and backup are available for an adverse event? Planning reduces ambiguity but cannot guarantee safety. If severe, sudden, or alarming symptoms are already present, call 9-1-1 or emergency services instead of completing the booking. What record will I receive? Request the date and time, substances and amounts, relevant identifiers or lot information, administering professional, observations, aftercare directions, and contact route. Ask how records are stored, corrected, shared with another clinician, and protected from a payer or event host. Clarify complaint and adverse-event reporting processes. What is the complete price and cancellation policy? Include assessment, travel, parking, taxes, supplies, additions, group minimums, after-hours fees, records, follow-up, and the outcome when care is declined clinically. Price transparency does not establish clinical quality, but hidden contingencies can distort consent. Before entering payment details, summarize the answers in six lines: accountable professional; assessment process; exact products; consent pathway; mobile safeguards; follow-up and escalation. Pause if any line remains empty or contradictory. A legitimate result of pre-booking questions is choosing another setting, seeking independent advice, using ordinary self-care when appropriate, or declining all commercial options. The checklist succeeds by making uncertainty visible before convenience and payment create momentum.

Questions this guide answers

Is “nurse administered” enough provider information?

No. Request full names, designations, registration details, clinical roles, and the professional basis for assessment, authorization, administration, and follow-up.

Should payment come before product disclosure?

Preferably obtain the exact proposed products, assessment process, consent terms, full charges, cancellation consequences, and professional identities before paying.

What if a provider will not answer pre-booking questions?

Pause the transaction, preserve the response, and seek clarification or independent advice; lack of an answer does not by itself prove misconduct.

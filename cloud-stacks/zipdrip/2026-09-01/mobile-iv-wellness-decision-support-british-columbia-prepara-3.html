mobile IV wellness decision support British Columbia: Preparation Checklist 2

A practical seven-part checklist for gathering health, product, consent, setting, record, and escalation information before an appointment.

Preparation should make the clinical conversation more accurate, not make administration inevitable. This seven-part checklist helps a reader assemble information, verify identities and products, test the mobile environment, and preserve freedom to pause. It deliberately avoids recommending a formula, dose, timing schedule, or wellness outcome. Complete each item before treating an appointment as settled, then bring the notes to the individual assessment. Missing information is a reason for another question, not a reason to guess. Reviewed 1 September 2026. This educational checklist is not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment guidance; verify clinical and regulatory material before publication and again before recurring monthly use.

Items 1–3: personal record, professional identity, and products

1. Build a current personal information record. List prescription medicines, over-the-counter products, vitamins, herbal products, other supplements, allergies, sensitivities, previous medication or infusion reactions, and relevant health information requested by the assessing professional. Include product names and amounts exactly as used when possible. Do not stop, start, or change a medicine merely to fit an appointment; direct medication questions to an appropriate prescriber or pharmacist. Note new symptoms or changes since booking so they can be reassessed. 2. Identify every responsible professional. Obtain the assessor’s and administrator’s names, designations, roles, and registration-check details. Ask who selects or authorizes the proposed medication, whether the nurse acts autonomously or with a client-specific order, and who is available if the plan changes. Professional titles should be verified rather than inferred from advertising. 3. Create a product worksheet. Request the complete ingredient list, concentration, route, brand or manufacturer information, product identifier, lot and expiry details available at the appropriate stage, and the policy for substitutions. Search the Drug Product Database for drug records and the Licensed Natural Health Products Database for natural health product records. Match exact products. A database result does not decide that intravenous use, a combination, or an advertised purpose is right for a person. Ask the clinician to check allergies, medicine and supplement interactions, contraindications, and uncertainties. Leave benefit claims unproven unless the correct current record and appropriate evidence support them.

Items 4–5: consent discussion and mobile environment

4. Prepare consent questions. Write down what you want explained: the proposed procedure, material risks, realistic limits, alternatives—including doing nothing or seeking another kind of care—expected monitoring, reasons the plan might stop, and what happens if consent is withdrawn. Ask when the discussion occurs and how it is documented. Payment, a group booking, or arrival of supplies does not eliminate the right to ask questions or decline. Confirm that any revised ingredient list triggers renewed explanation and agreement. 5. Inspect the intended setting without attempting to perform a clinical safety assessment yourself. Identify a private area where health information cannot be overheard, stable seating or a place to recline, adequate light, handwashing access, a clean surface, manageable pets and children, minimal interruptions, and a clear route for entry and exit. Ask the provider what infection-prevention controls and supplies are required, how sharps and waste leave the site, and who decides the location is unacceptable. In an office, hotel, wedding, party, or corporate setting, protect individual privacy from hosts and organizers. Avoid scheduling around pressure to return immediately to driving, work, alcohol use, exercise, or another activity; ask the responsible clinician for applicable instructions. Convenience cannot substitute for an environment that permits assessment, monitoring, documentation, and emergency access.

Items 6–7: records, aftercare, and the stop rule

6. Arrange the information trail. Keep the written quote, cancellation and refund terms, clinician identifiers, product worksheet, consent material, receipt, and aftercare instructions. Ask what the clinical record will contain, how to request a copy, how privacy is protected in a mobile setting, and who receives a report of a concern. Confirm a direct contact route for non-emergency questions after departure. Written instructions should distinguish expected follow-up from circumstances requiring prompt medical attention. Ask how an adverse event would be managed, including stopping criteria, monitoring, available equipment, consultation, emergency activation, documentation, and later follow-up. 7. Apply a firm stop rule. Do not proceed while professional identity, authority, ingredients, interactions, consent, environmental suitability, documentation, or escalation arrangements remain materially unclear. Never use a commercial IV wellness visit to investigate serious symptoms. Call 9-1-1 or emergency services for severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, loss of consciousness, stroke signs, rapidly deteriorating symptoms, or any situation believed to be an emergency. Seek an appropriate healthcare professional when diagnosis or treatment is needed but the situation is not emergent. Package names involving hydration, immune support, energy, recovery, hangovers, athletic performance, NAD+, detox, weight loss, or anti-aging do not override this rule. Finishing the checklist means being ready for a better assessment, not being cleared for administration or guaranteed an outcome.

Questions this guide answers

Does completing the checklist confirm suitability?

No. It prepares information for individualized assessment; it does not authorize administration or predict benefit.

Should prescribed medication be changed before an appointment?

Do not alter medication merely to accommodate a booking. Ask the appropriate prescriber or pharmacist about personal medication decisions.

What if the provider proposes a substitute ingredient?

Pause for exact product verification, updated interaction and allergy review, an explanation of material changes, and renewed consent.

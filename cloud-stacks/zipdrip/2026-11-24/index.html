From the publisher

A booking may involve a scheduling person, a professional who reviews health information, and the person expected to attend. Before a mobile IV wellness appointment in British Columbia, make space for those roles in the same set of notes. Write each question next to the person who can answer it and leave room to record when an answer has been confirmed. This guide provides general information, not medical advice. It was reviewed on 23 November 2026.

Start with the decision you are making

Some people are only checking availability. Others already have a date and are trying to understand what they have agreed to. State your stage directly when you reach out. Ask whether the conversation reserves a time, collects background information, or arranges an assessment with someone who can discuss proposed care. A reply about one stage can leave the next stage entirely open. If you would need an answer before sharing a phone number or making a payment, put that question first rather than assuming the form will explain it later.

Route health questions to an identified professional

Ask for the role and name of the person you can speak with about your own circumstances. Find out whether that person reviews your information before arrival or during the appointment. Explain the kind of information you need to discuss, such as a medication list or past reaction, and ask for the appropriate way to share it. A booking representative can tell you how to reach the appropriate professional. Product selection, questions about suitability, and the relevance of a health history need a discussion with the practitioner who has the information and responsibility to answer.

Make the proposed service visible

Ask how you will find out what is actually proposed, who made that decision, and how you can ask questions before consenting. An advertised service category may still leave the contents or timing undecided. Write down the product name and intended next step only after the professional has explained them. If the explanation relies on a description you have never seen, ask to see or receive that description in a form you can read. This is a way to prepare for the conversation. Keep individual decisions about medicines, ingredients, allergies and interactions with the practitioner reviewing your circumstances.

Preserve room to pause

Set a point before the visit when you will check for missing answers. That point can be earlier than the payment deadline if you need time to contact another care provider. Ask what happens to a reservation when the assessment is deferred, the plan changes, or you decide to decline. BCCNM's consent standard describes the right to withdraw consent. An open time slot or a completed intake can be useful administrative milestones while a decision about care is still pending. Let the care team know that the decision is pending so it can explain the next step.

Know who holds the record

Ask how to get a copy of the appointment record and whom to contact about an omission or a correction. Find out where the practitioner will record the discussion, the information that informed the decision, any changes, and any plan after the appointment. A printed charge receipt and a clinical record answer different questions. If you need information for another care provider, ask how the service can provide it securely. Retain a short list of contact names and roles for future questions rather than trying to reconstruct the conversation from promotional messages.

Plan for a different outcome

Imagine the professional concludes there should be no procedure that day. Ask how the decision will be documented, what follow-up the practitioner suggests, and whom you can call with questions. Ask the provider to explain its plan if someone becomes unwell during or after a visit and how urgent assistance would be reached. In an emergency, call 9-1-1 or local emergency services. The most useful record from a booking may be a clear decision to pause, with a named contact and a documented route for further questions. You can make that plan even while the appointment date is provisional.

Questions this publication answers

Who can explain which appointment information is still missing?

Ask who handles intake and which practitioner is responsible for reviewing your information. Keep scheduling and payment questions with the booking contact so you can get a clear answer about each part of the process.

Can I ask for more time before deciding about a visit?

Yes. Ask the practitioner what they have proposed and whether you can continue the conversation later. Ask the booking contact what that would mean for the date or any payment already arranged.

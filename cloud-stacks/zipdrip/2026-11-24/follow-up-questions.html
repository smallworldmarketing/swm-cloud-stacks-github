Questions to Keep for Follow-Up After an Assessment

Create a useful follow-up note with the right contact, the assessment date, unanswered questions and options for obtaining records.

After a conversation, you may remember a question you did not ask, need a copy of the record, or want to clarify a statement before deciding about a future visit. Gather the details that will help the relevant person answer. Put any urgent concerns on the right escalation route. This is general information, not medical advice. The guide was reviewed on 23 November 2026.

Who should receive each kind of question

Write down the practitioner contact and the booking or billing contact separately. Identify whether you are asking about an existing record, the assessment, a proposed product, an administrative charge, or another appointment. Ask the provider where to send a question that includes private health details. If you are unsure which professional is responsible, describe the type of question without disclosing unnecessary information and ask to be referred to the right person. Keep track of the expected reply date for routine questions and which service channel you used.

What details will help identify the discussion

Start with the date, the people involved and the part of the conversation you want to clarify. If the practitioner used a product name, write it as it appears in the record or ask how to verify it. Describe where your understanding became uncertain and ask the professional to explain it in familiar terms. If newer health information relates to the question, ask how to share it. Avoid copying an entire previous intake into a general message when a private explanation or a narrow record request would address what you need.

Which part of the record can you request

Ask how to get an appropriate copy of the record, what details you need to identify it, and who handles questions if something is missing. You can ask where a proposed service, a change or a decision to pause was documented. If you are comparing a receipt with notes from an assessment, ask whether the records serve different purposes and whom to contact about each one. If you have identified an error, describe the specific entry and ask for the provider's process for reviewing your concern.

What would you want to clarify before another decision

If the earlier discussion left a decision pending, ask whether the explanation you received still applies and what information a professional would need now. Ask about responsibility for reviewing medication information, allergies or a change in circumstances. You can ask who will explain any updated proposal and how the discussion will be documented. BCCNM's consent standard recognizes a person's ability to decline or withdraw consent. If you prefer to seek more information first, ask how to pause the booking and what will happen to any current reservation.

How should a reply be shared with someone helping you

Decide whether you want to share the answer with a support person, payer or another practitioner. Request a format that helps you use the information, and ask the provider how to share private material appropriately if needed. For example, you may share confirmation of a changed booking while keeping a discussion about assessment private. Keep the date and speaker with the answer. If the reply gives a different account from what you remember, ask for clarification and save both versions clearly so you can identify the difference.

What should you do if the concern is urgent

Ask the practitioner to explain in advance where to turn if a concern arises after a visit or assessment. Routine questions about records, a future date, or a payment can follow the agreed contact route. In an emergency, call 9-1-1 or local emergency services. HealthLink BC identifies severe dehydration as a medical emergency. Keep the address or location and any information responders request available. Questions that can wait can be documented for follow-up once urgent needs have been addressed by the appropriate service.

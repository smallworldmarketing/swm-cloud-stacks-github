Las Vegas direct primary care membership and treatment claim eva: Cost and Scope Questions

A contract checklist for documenting membership fees, exclusions, optional services, outside expenses, cancellation rules, and uncertain treatment costs.

Use this checklist when the immediate decision is whether a proposed membership has a sufficiently clear scope and cost structure to review further. It does not estimate savings or decide whether care is medically appropriate. Figures and inclusions can change, so obtain current written terms instead of copying an advertisement. This independent material is informational, not medical advice, and was reviewed August 24, 2026. Keep emergency and urgent care planning separate: potentially serious symptoms require timely medical help, not a discussion about fees or a wait for routine messaging.

Mark every contractual cost and service boundary

Write the legal names of the member and every contracting entity at the top of the worksheet. Then transcribe the effective date, minimum term, billing interval, automatic-renewal rule, payment method, notice address, cancellation deadline, refund conditions, and consequences of a missed payment. Ask which document controls if a website, enrollment page, spoken explanation, and signed agreement conflict. Save the version presented on the date you are considering enrollment. Create columns for included, extra charge, excluded, and unclear. Place each promised function into one column: office visits, virtual visits, portal messages, preventive discussions, care coordination, forms, records work, laboratory collection, specimen processing, medication management, or after-hours contact. A statement that members receive access does not necessarily mean a service has no separate fee. Ask whether frequency, duration, location, clinician availability, eligibility, or clinical appropriateness limits an inclusion. Request an amendment or authoritative written answer for material ambiguities. Build a separate expense table for services that other organizations may provide. Include emergency transport, emergency departments, hospital care, specialists, imaging centers, laboratories, retail or compounding pharmacies, medical equipment, and outside procedures. For each, note who bills, whether insurance may be submitted, whether preauthorization could apply, and who handles denied claims. Do not record a hypothetical discount as a saving. Only compare amounts for the same service, date, setting, and terms, and recognize that future use is uncertain. Optional treatment categories need individual rows rather than a single wellness label. If functional, hormone, weight-management, IV, peptide, regenerative, light-based, hair, or supplement services are discussed, ask whether the consultation, testing, product, administration, monitoring, supplies, and follow-up are separately priced. Do not infer the existence, current availability, or price of any service from this checklist. Record estimates as estimates and identify what could change them.

Calculate commitment without turning it into a treatment choice

Choose a review horizon that matches the contract, then total only known mandatory payments. Keep recurring membership charges apart from one-time enrollment costs and optional clinical expenses. Use ranges for uncertain items, label taxes or shipping when applicable, and do not substitute a best-case figure for missing information. If cancellation requires advance notice, calculate the latest notice date and possible payments during that period. Ask what happens to prepaid balances, pending laboratory work, undelivered products, future appointments, and records access after termination. Evaluate nonfinancial scope next. Identify the addresses where included care can occur, the allowed appointment channels, who may respond, routine response expectations, after-hours limitations, and backup-care instructions. Determine whether the agreement promises a service or merely provides a way to request it. Ask what happens during clinician leave, technical outages, travel, relocation, or closure. Never treat routine portal access as an emergency plan. Before agreeing to an optional intervention, request a written proposal naming the exact product or procedure, responsible clinician, dispensing or supplying organization, monitoring elements, anticipated schedule, alternatives, material uncertainties, and foreseeable reasons for additional expense. Financial consent should not be combined with an assumption of clinical benefit. A lower quoted price, package, or membership inclusion cannot establish safety, effectiveness, necessity, or suitability. Close the worksheet with three tests. First, can another person identify what is owed and when? Second, can you distinguish included services from external bills? Third, are cancellation and unresolved-cost risks tolerable without relying on hoped-for outcomes? If any answer is no, pause and request clarification. Keep the completed checklist with the agreement, dated quotes, receipts, insurer correspondence, and product documentation. For medical choices, take the material to a qualified professional who is independent of the sale when a second opinion would help.

Questions this guide answers

Should optional treatments be included in the membership total?

List mandatory membership charges separately. Itemize optional consultations, tests, products, procedures, monitoring, supplies, shipping, and follow-up so uncertainty remains visible.

Is advertised access the same as an included appointment?

Not necessarily. Ask the agreement to clarify eligibility, frequency, location, duration, staffing, clinical limits, and separate charges.

Check the Agreement Before Paying for a Membership

Use this checklist to identify the signing parties, recurring obligations, included services, service locations, and steps for ending a membership.

When reviewing a membership, keep the contract and its attachments together. A welcome page can help you identify which questions to put on paper, and the signed agreement can tell you which version applies to your enrollment. Read each section with a concrete example in mind. If a provision depends on the clinician, location, or type of service, ask the practice to identify the relevant term in writing. A complete agreement review also gives you a record to consult when a charge, change of address, or cancellation question comes up.

Editorial date November 24, 2026. This guide offers general planning information. Ask qualified professionals about individual medical, legal, or coverage decisions.

Identify the parties, enrollee and starting date

Find the organization that will sign and collect payments. If a public-facing brand differs from the name on the signature line, ask the enrollment contact how the two names relate and which one will appear on receipts. Record exactly who is included and the date the membership begins. For a household arrangement, ask whether each person signs separately or has a different start date. Write down how the practice confirms enrollment and what you can expect to receive after signing. Keep this information handy for any later question about a charge or the identity of the practice.

Read the fee schedule in billing order

Follow the paperwork from the first payment through a typical renewal. If the practice lists an enrollment charge, monthly charge or prepaid term, record when each is due and where the amount appears in writing. Ask how the practice sends notices about price changes and which contact answers billing questions. Keep a separate line for travel, outside testing, supplies, or administrative fees that could appear under another part of the contract. Use actual documents to fill in amounts. An unanswered line is a question to take back to the practice before projecting your monthly expenses.

Describe included services using specific examples

Go through the service schedule one line at a time. If the document states that visits are included, ask who can provide the visit, where it can take place and how the practice decides which appointment category applies. Ask how requests for a separate test, outside specialist or message after hours would be handled. Write each answer next to the specific term or attachment that covers it. Take special care to record when a service is furnished or billed by someone outside the practice. A clear service example can surface a question a broad membership label leaves open.

Check locations and handling of changes

Find the address or visit setting named in the agreement. Ask whether location changes, remote contact or travel to another area are addressed in writing, and how the practice communicates a change. If a family member needs a particular location, record which service depends on that address and the date you asked about it. Keep the clinic contact that can confirm an appointment at the listed site. Ask who would answer your question if a clinician changes, the location moves, or you are unable to attend in person. The office can point you to current arrangements.

Find the cancellation and records instructions now

Read the section on ending or renewing the agreement while you still have time to compare terms. Note any required notice method, the dates that matter, who receives the request and how the practice confirms it. Identify any language about refunds, final charges and care already scheduled. Ask how to request copies of records and how the practice coordinates an ongoing matter if the membership ends. Save the notice address and the records contact together. If an important term remains hard to interpret, keep its exact wording with your question for the practice or a qualified adviser.

Where a term links to another document, request the linked document and record its version. If earlier conversations suggested a different term, ask for the practice’s current written answer and save the reply with the agreement.

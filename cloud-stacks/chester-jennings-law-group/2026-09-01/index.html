From the publisher

An injury incident can create several urgent but separate problems: health and safety, record gathering, insurance correspondence, and decisions about professional help. Treating them as separate workstreams makes the situation easier to organize without deciding fault or predicting a result. This overview presents a practical sequence for Georgia residents, especially those in the Atlanta area. It supplies general information rather than legal, medical, insurance, or financial advice. Rules involving notice, deadlines, parties, evidence, coverage, liens, and court procedure vary with the facts and can change. A licensed Georgia lawyer and the appropriate health, insurance, or public authority should address case-specific questions promptly.

Stabilize the situation and build a neutral record

Health and immediate safety come first. Seek appropriate emergency assistance when needed and follow advice from qualified medical professionals. Once immediate needs are addressed, begin a factual incident timeline. Record dates, approximate times, locations, people present, reports requested, appointments, expenses, and communications. Separate what you personally observed from what another person told you. Avoid assigning blame in the timeline; its purpose is memory support and file navigation. Create an inventory rather than one undifferentiated folder. Useful categories may include incident reports, lawfully obtained photographs, witness contact details, medical records, bills, pharmacy documents, wage or leave records, transportation receipts, property information, and correspondence. Keep originals intact when practical, note where each item came from, and preserve electronic files with recognizable names and backup copies. Do not enter restricted property, interfere with another person’s records, impersonate anyone, or use surveillance tactics to obtain material. For an auto incident, record the agency associated with a report and the identifier provided, if any. A report is a record, not a judicial finding about legal responsibility. Georgia Department of Public Safety open-records guidance, reviewed September 1, 2026, explains how requests may be submitted to that department; it does not establish that DPS holds every crash record. Confirm the correct custodian before requesting anything. Maintain an insurance log containing notice dates, claim or reference numbers, the organization and representative involved, the communication channel, and any requested follow-up. Preserve the exact documents received. Before answering a consequential request or signing an authorization, release, settlement document, or other agreement, obtain advice suited to the document and circumstances. This guide does not tell a reader to provide or decline a statement, accept or reject an offer, or sign or refuse any paper.

Compare lawyers through verifiable questions

Attorney selection should begin with verification, not advertising. Search the State Bar of Georgia member directory using identifying information and confirm that the record matches the person being considered. Names can be similar, so compare the displayed details carefully. The directory is a verification resource, not an endorsement or prediction of competence. The State Bar’s disciplinary-process material can help readers understand what public regulatory information means and how the process operates. Both resources were reviewed September 1, 2026. Next, compare the proposed working relationship. Ask what types of matters the lawyer is presently prepared to discuss, who would supervise the file, which tasks may be handled by other team members, and how questions are routed. Request an explanation of communication practices without assuming a promised response time. Ask how the lawyer separates preliminary information gathering from a decision about representation. Sending an inquiry does not necessarily create an attorney-client relationship, so avoid transmitting unnecessary sensitive details before understanding the intake channel and confidentiality arrangements. Read any proposed engagement agreement in full. Questions can cover the contingency percentage and whether it changes at defined stages; which expenses may arise; who advances costs; whether repayment could be required; how medical or other liens are handled; what happens if representation ends; and how the client receives a copy of the file. This publication supplies no fee percentage or cost-allocation claim. The written agreement and the lawyer’s explanation control the proposed arrangement. Recognize when individualized review matters

Some facts make prompt legal review especially important even though this overview cannot calculate a deadline. Examples include uncertainty about the incident date; a possible government entity or employee; a minor or legally incapacitated person; death; multiple vehicles, businesses, property controllers, employers, or coverage sources; a request for a recorded statement; an authorization with broad language; a proposed release; or approaching correspondence dates. Exceptions, notice requirements, responsible parties, venue, and limitation periods require fact-specific analysis. Different incident settings also create different record questions. A commercial-truck event may involve a driver, vehicle owner, carrier, maintenance provider, shipper, or other business, but their presence does not establish responsibility. A rideshare inquiry may require accurate trip-status and platform records. A premises incident raises questions about the exact location and who controlled it. Motorcycle, pedestrian, bicycle, dog-bite, catastrophic-injury, wrongful-death, and assault-related inquiries each require their own safety, privacy, authority, medical, and coverage analysis. If a civil case is filed, filing, service, pleadings, discovery, depositions, expert work, mediation, trial, and appeal are distinct stages. Georgia Courts’ self-help resources and electronic-access information, reviewed September 1, 2026, can orient readers to public court resources. They are not substitutes for counsel and cannot determine what a particular person should file. Public access also does not mean every document is available online or appropriate to redistribute. Keep the file usable throughout the process. Save dated copies of correspondence, retain signed agreements and amendments, reconcile document inventories periodically, and record where originals are held. At closeout, request and organize the final settlement statement or other closing accounting when applicable, lien-related records, cost information, executed documents, and returned file materials. Retain them according to advice appropriate to the matter. Good organization cannot establish liability or value, but it can make questions clearer and reduce preventable confusion.

Questions this publication answers

Does an incident report decide who was legally responsible?

No. It may document observations and reported information, but legal responsibility requires a separate, fact-specific analysis.

Does contacting a law office automatically create representation?

Not necessarily. Ask how the office forms an attorney-client relationship and when confidentiality applies before sending unnecessary sensitive material.

Can this overview calculate a Georgia deadline?

No. Dates, exceptions, notice provisions, parties, and procedural choices require prompt review by a licensed Georgia lawyer.

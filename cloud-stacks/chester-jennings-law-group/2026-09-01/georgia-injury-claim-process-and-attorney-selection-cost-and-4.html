Georgia injury claim process and attorney selection: Cost and Scope Questions 3

Seven practical subjects to clarify when reading a Georgia representation agreement, including fees, expenses, liens, staffing, and file return.

A cost conversation should produce an understandable map of responsibilities, not a predicted net recovery. The important document is the proposed representation agreement together with any incorporated terms or later amendments. This list offers seven subjects to discuss with a licensed Georgia lawyer before deciding whether an arrangement fits. It does not state a customary percentage, decide who must bear an expense, or interpret a particular contract. Those details vary, and the written language controls only as applicable under governing law and the circumstances. Ask for explanations in plain language, take notes, and distinguish an estimate from a binding term. No answer about fees can guarantee acceptance, settlement, litigation, duration, or outcome.

Seven subjects that reveal the financial structure

1. Fee calculation: ask what event or amount supplies the base for a contingent fee and whether the calculation can change at defined stages. Request a worked hypothetical using invented round numbers, explicitly labelled as an illustration rather than a prediction. Confirm that the explanation matches the written clause. 2. Case expenses: identify categories that might arise, such as records, filing, service, depositions, experts, travel, or copying. Ask who may authorize an expense, whether client approval is needed above any stated threshold, how spending is reported, and what the agreement says if representation ends or no money is obtained. Do not infer an answer from advertising. 3. Medical balances and liens: ask whether the office investigates asserted reimbursement interests or outstanding balances, what work is included, and whether separate professionals might be involved. A lawyer can explain the legal scope; providers and responsible administrators can explain their records. 4. Ending representation: locate provisions addressing withdrawal, discharge, attorney liens, earned compensation, outstanding expenses, file transfer, and deadlines during transition. Ask how these clauses interact, without assuming that ending the relationship erases obligations. 5. Division of work: determine whether other lawyers, outside counsel, vendors, or referring lawyers may participate and how any division affects responsibility or the stated fee. 6. Accounting: ask what closing statement or periodic ledger will show, how disputed entries are handled, and when supporting records may be requested. 7. Changes: identify who can amend the agreement, how consent is documented, and whether side conversations have any effect. These topics form a reading framework, not a ranking system.

Scope questions that belong beside cost questions

Financial terms make little sense without knowing the work included. Ask whether the proposed engagement covers only an insurance claim, also permits filing a lawsuit, or requires another decision before litigation. Clarify who evaluates possible defendants, coverage issues, venue, government involvement, appeals, probate matters, workers’ compensation questions, or related proceedings. Do not assume a broad practice description means every task is included. Find the clause defining the client, especially when a child, estate, family member, business, guardian, or multiple injured people may be involved. Ask which decisions remain the client’s, which steps counsel may take routinely, and how material choices will be presented. Discuss communication mechanics: primary contact, backup contact, expected method for routine updates, urgent escalation route, document portal, accessibility needs, and procedures when contact information changes. No universal response interval should be invented, but the office should be able to explain its own process. Ask how confidential data is stored and which vendors may receive it. Sensitive assault-related, medical, immigration, employment, or family information deserves purposeful handling rather than indiscriminate intake. Also clarify exclusions. Property damage, benefits appeals, tax advice, financial planning, criminal matters, protective orders, family disputes, and lien resolution should not be presumed included merely because they relate to the same event. When another professional is needed, ask who is responsible for finding that person and whether any separate agreement may apply.

A disciplined way to compare proposed agreements

Create one worksheet per office and copy language rather than paraphrasing from memory. Use columns for fee trigger, expense responsibility, authorization, scope, exclusions, staffing, communication, termination, file return, dispute procedure, and closing accounting. Add a final column for questions that remain unanswered. Comparability matters more than reducing everything to one number. A lower stated percentage, considered alone, says nothing about expense treatment, included work, staffing, or termination provisions. Conversely, a detailed explanation is not an outcome promise. Review any revised document from the beginning; changes in one clause may affect another. Keep the version received and the date, and do not send one office another person’s confidential proposal without appropriate permission. The State Bar of Georgia publishes official information about lawyer referral services and the disciplinary process. As of September 1, 2026, those pages can help readers understand official channels, but they do not interpret a private fee contract or choose counsel. Direct contract questions to the lawyer proposing the agreement, and consider independent Georgia legal advice if a material term remains unclear. Do not sign because of artificial pressure, nor treat this article as instructions to refuse a document. The decision is whether the language, explanation, scope, and working process are sufficiently understood to make an informed choice. Preserve the final signed version and later amendments with permanent records if representation begins.

Questions this guide answers

Does this guide provide a typical contingency percentage?

No. It does not supply or imply a fee percentage; readers should examine the proposed written agreement and ask the lawyer to explain it.

Why compare scope and cost together?

A fee cannot be evaluated sensibly without knowing which work, stages, people, and related issues the engagement includes or excludes.

Should an advertisement be used to infer expense responsibility?

No. Expense authorization and allocation should be verified in the applicable written terms and discussed with the proposing lawyer.

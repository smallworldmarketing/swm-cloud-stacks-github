Georgia injury claim process and attorney selection: Questions Before Booking 3

Eight screening questions to ask before booking a Georgia injury-law consultation, with attention to scope, privacy, conflicts, and preparation.

A booking call should establish whether a consultation is worth scheduling and how to participate safely. It is not the time to transmit every record, seek an outcome prediction, or assume representation has begun. The eight questions below test administrative fit: the individual lawyer’s identity, relevant subject scope, conflict process, confidentiality, secure document handling, meeting participants, preparation, and follow-up. They do not ask an office to promise acceptance, availability, speed, value, or success. Keep the initial description short and neutral, especially when the event involves sensitive medical information, a death, an assault, a child, an estate, workplace concerns, or several potentially involved people.

Questions one through four: identity, scope, conflicts, privacy

1. Which lawyer would conduct the consultation, and how is the lawyer’s full name spelled? This allows a separate official-directory check and prevents confusion between a lawyer, intake worker, investigator, or vendor. As of September 1, 2026, the State Bar of Georgia member directory is the appropriate approved source for public membership verification. 2. Does the office consider consultations involving this general incident category and the types of parties already known? Ask without demanding a case evaluation. A car collision, commercial-truck event, motorcycle incident, rideshare trip, controlled premises, dog bite, catastrophic injury, death, or assault-related inquiry may call for different intake information. A general “personal injury” label does not define the proposed scope. 3. What minimum information is needed for conflict screening, and when will the office say whether the screening is complete? Provide names and roles through the method the office requests, but avoid a narrative containing unnecessary confidential details. Conflict screening is not acceptance. 4. How does the office treat information supplied before representation is confirmed, and which channel should be used for sensitive material? Ask about portals, email, text messages, third-party intake systems, retention, deletion, and authorized recipients. Never send passwords, unrequested intimate images, full financial credentials, or unrelated health records merely because a generic upload button exists. If immediate personal safety is involved, contact the appropriate emergency, medical, law-enforcement, or support resource rather than waiting for a booking response.

Questions five through eight: participants, documents, access, next steps

5. Who will attend, and what role does each person have? Determine whether the meeting is with a lawyer, staff member, interpreter, outside participant, or multiple people. Do not assume credentials, language support, or decision authority; ask directly. 6. Which documents, if any, should be available for the first conversation? A useful answer may identify a short chronology, relevant correspondence, report identifiers, insurance information, medical-provider list, and documents bearing stated dates. Confirm whether copies are sufficient and preserve originals. Do not obtain additional material through trespass, unauthorized device access, surveillance, or interference. 7. What accessibility and communication arrangements can the office provide for the meeting? Discuss the preferred medium, physical or remote access, captioning or other needs, support-person participation, privacy at each location, and a backup plan if technology fails. This is a process question, not permission to claim an unverified service. 8. What happens after the consultation? Ask whether the office provides a written engagement proposal, requests more information, communicates that it will not proceed, or uses another defined step. Find out who sends that message and through which channel. No one should infer representation from silence, a calendar invitation, or document upload. If time-sensitive facts may exist, state that concern plainly and seek prompt Georgia legal advice; a future appointment does not itself protect rights or resolve a deadline.

Use the answers as a booking filter

After the call, label each answer clear, incomplete, or requiring lawyer discussion. Clear means the process is understandable, not that the office is necessarily suitable. Incomplete means a precise follow-up can resolve the gap. Some legal questions properly wait for the lawyer rather than the scheduler, but the office should distinguish scheduling information from legal advice. Compare offices on the same factors: verified identity, subject-scope response, conflict procedure, privacy explanation, secure transfer, meeting participants, requested preparation, accessibility, and written next steps. Do not compare advertised results, invented scores, supposed insider influence, or promised claim values. Also avoid forcing every incident into one template. For example, a wrongful-death consultation may require questions about who has legal authority and family privacy; an assault-related inquiry may prioritize safety and controlled disclosure; a trucking or rideshare event may involve several organizations and record sources. Those differences affect preparation, not predicted outcomes. Keep booking notes factual and private. If the scheduler asks for more information than seems necessary, request an explanation and a secure alternative rather than disclosing impulsively. If the office cannot address a needed administrative feature, that is a fit consideration, not proof of wrongdoing. The booking decision is whether the meeting has a clear purpose, appropriate participants, manageable privacy practices, and a defined next step. The later retention decision requires separate review of scope, written terms, fees, expenses, staffing, and communication responsibilities.

Questions this guide answers

Does a scheduled consultation mean an office has accepted the matter?

No. Scheduling, conflict screening, and document intake do not by themselves establish representation or case acceptance.

How much sensitive information should be sent while booking?

Use a minimal neutral description and ask about confidentiality, conflicts, secure transfer, retention, and recipients before sending sensitive records.

What should be verified once the consulting lawyer is named?

Check the individual lawyer’s public membership information through the official State Bar of Georgia member directory.

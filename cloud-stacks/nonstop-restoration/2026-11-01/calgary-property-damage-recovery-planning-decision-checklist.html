Calgary Property Recovery: Your Next-Step Decision Checklist

A practical checklist for deciding what to do next after property damage in Calgary, from safety checks to documentation before contacting providers.

After a water, fire, mould, or sewage event, the hardest part is often not the damage itself but deciding what to do in the next few hours and days. This checklist is built around the decision an owner, tenant, or manager actually faces right after discovering damage: what must happen first, what can wait, and what to document before anyone starts cleanup or repair work. It does not replace guidance from emergency responders, your insurer, or a qualified contractor, and it does not tell you to enter a building that authorities or your insurer have not cleared.

The goal is sequencing. Many recovery mistakes happen because someone skips a step, such as throwing out damaged items before photographing them, or starting cleanup before confirming the building is safe to occupy. Working through this checklist in order helps avoid those gaps and keeps your options open for insurance, repair, and future reference.

Before You Touch Anything: Safety and Access Checks

Confirm life-safety status first. If there is any risk of fire, gas, electrical hazard, structural instability, sewage contamination, or trauma and biohazard exposure, the City of Calgary's recovery guidance is clear that you should not enter damaged buildings or restricted areas until authorities say it is safe, and that injuries often occur when people move through damaged areas or debris (calgary.ca/emergencies/recovery.html). Call 911 for immediate danger, and use 311 for other City of Calgary services if you need guidance on air quality or building status.

Once entry is confirmed safe, the City also advises waiting at least 24 hours after an incident before moving freely through a damaged home, wearing hard-soled footwear, gloves, and breathing protection, and being cautious of soft floors, exposed nails, and electrical wires (calgary.ca/emergencies/cleaning-repairs.html). Do not touch damaged utilities or electrical equipment yourself; that is a job for the utility provider or a licensed trade.

If asbestos is suspected in older building materials disturbed by the damage, stop work in that area. Alberta's Occupational Health and Safety Code restricts work in areas where airborne asbestos may exceed exposure limits to workers holding a valid asbestos worker certificate, and employers must give 72 hours notice before starting asbestos projects (alberta.ca/asbestos-worker-training). This is a decision for a qualified assessor, not a visual judgment made on-site.

Documentation and Contact Order: What to Do Once It's Safe

Document before you clean, discard, or repair anything. Alberta's disaster recovery guidance lists photographing all damaged items inside and outside the property as the first recovery step, done before throwing anything away or starting repairs (alberta.ca/steps-to-take-following-a-disaster). The City of Calgary echoes this, recommending photos and videos before, during, and after cleanup, plus a written list of damaged or discarded items and saved receipts for cleanup and repair costs (calgary.ca/emergencies/recovery.html).

Contact your insurer early, but do not assume what they will or will not cover. Alberta's guidance recommends opening a claim and requesting a letter from your insurer outlining coverage and exclusions, since that letter becomes part of your own record regardless of how the claim proceeds (alberta.ca/steps-to-take-following-a-disaster). A restoration contractor can document conditions and scope of work, but only your insurer can confirm what is covered.

Decide whether the damage is within your own capability before doing any repair yourself. The City's cleaning and repairs guidance suggests asking whether you know the full extent of the repairs, whether you have the skills and tools, whether a building or trades permit is required, and whether your insurance will actually cover work you do yourself (calgary.ca/emergencies/cleaning-repairs.html). Anything beyond minor smoke or water damage is flagged as a case where contacting a professional restoration company is worth considering. Services commonly described in this category include water extraction, structural drying, mould remediation, sewage cleanup, fire and smoke restoration, and reconstruction; Nonstop Restoration is one Calgary-based provider describing this scope of work (nonstoprestoration.ca), though reviewing more than one provider's scope and documentation practices is a reasonable next step rather than a single default choice.

Finally, check whether permits or inspections apply before repairs begin. Any renovation changing the structure of your home requires a building permit, and the City of Calgary's homeowner inspection system is used to book and manage building, electrical, plumbing, and development inspections (calgary.ca/development/homeowner-inspections.html). Confirming this before reconstruction starts avoids rework later.

Questions this guide answers

What should I document before cleanup starts?

Photograph and video all damaged items and areas, inside and outside, before throwing anything away or beginning repairs, as recommended in Alberta's post-disaster guidance. Keep a written list of discarded or damaged items and save every receipt tied to cleanup and repair. This record supports your insurance claim regardless of which contractor you eventually choose.

When is it safe to re-enter a damaged property?

Re-entry is a decision for authorities, not a personal judgment call; the City of Calgary advises not entering damaged buildings or restricted areas until officials confirm it is safe, and waiting at least 24 hours after an incident even once entry is allowed. If you are unsure about air quality, 311 can arrange measurement. This caution applies most strongly after fire, structural damage, or sewage exposure.

Do I need a permit before starting repairs?

Any renovation that changes your home's structure requires a building permit, and plumbing, electrical, or gas repairs typically require licensed trades under Alberta rules. The City of Calgary's inspections system is used to book required building, electrical, and plumbing inspections once a permit is issued. Confirming this before work starts avoids having to redo completed repairs.

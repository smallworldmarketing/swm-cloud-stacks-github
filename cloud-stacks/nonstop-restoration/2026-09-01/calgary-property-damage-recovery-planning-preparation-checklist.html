Calgary property damage recovery planning: Preparation Checklist

Prepare contacts, access rules, records, inventories, and approval paths before Calgary water or flood damage turns decisions into confusion.

Preparation cannot prevent every loss, but it can prevent basic information from becoming another casualty. A useful property-recovery file identifies people, authority, records, and safe communication routes before water enters a unit or flooding disrupts a building. It should never encourage occupants to investigate hazards or perform cleanup. Instead, it helps them withdraw, notify the right parties, protect information, and support authorized professionals. This decision framework is suitable for homes, rental properties, commercial premises, and multi-unit buildings when adapted to actual ownership and management responsibilities.

Build the authority and contact card

Create a short contact card for emergency services, utility emergency lines, insurer or broker, owner, landlord, condominium or strata contact where applicable, property manager, building security, and key trades. Verify it periodically and keep a protected copy somewhere other than the property. Do not pre-authorize anyone to enter against emergency or building restrictions. The card should state that life safety and official access directions override property-protection plans. Map who may make decisions for emergency spending, contractor engagement, unit access, common property, tenant communication, contents, and reconstruction. Include an alternate for absences. For a commercial site, identify the occupational health and safety contact and processes relevant to workers. Authority should be documented, not assumed from whoever answers the phone first. Prepare a property facts sheet containing the address, unit identifiers, ownership entity, insurer contact, policy number stored securely, shutoff locations for qualified personnel, alarm and access arrangements, known building drawings, major renovations, and specialist reports. This record is informational; it must not direct an unqualified occupant to operate gas, electricity, water, fire systems, or damaged equipment. Add communication rules. Specify the official incident channel, log keeper, privacy restrictions, and approval method. Personal, medical, trauma-related, tenant, and security information should reach only people who need it. A group chat may coordinate activity, but the permanent file should retain decisions and attachments in an organized form.

Prepare the evidence structure, not a hazard inspection

Create folders for incident chronology, photographs, videos, access directions, emergency contacts, insurer messages, estimates, authorizations, invoices, contents, specialist reports, permits, inspections, and closeout. Use filenames that include date, location, and subject. Preserve originals and work from copies when adding labels. Maintain a simple index so a later reviewer can find the evidence without relying on one person’s memory. Develop a pre-loss contents inventory using safe, ordinary access. Record rooms, significant items, serial numbers, purchase records, and photographs. Store a backup securely off-site or in an appropriately protected digital service. Do not collect sensitive identifiers that are unnecessary. For multi-unit properties, keep owner, tenant, and common-property records separated. Prepare blank incident forms. A photo log should have image number, date, photographer, authorized location, direction, and factual description. An action log should record time, caller, instruction, action, and follow-up. A contents sheet can capture item, owner, original room, apparent condition, container identifier, and disposition status. A contractor register should list scope, contact, access period, insurance or credential documents requested by the owner, and authorization status. These forms are for observations and custody, not amateur diagnosis. Avoid labels such as “clean,” “mould-free,” “asbestos,” or “structurally safe” unless properly attributed to a qualified source. Write “dark staining observed on north wall” rather than declaring its identity.

Choose decision triggers for water and flood events

Draft a first-hour card with four gates. Gate one asks whether anyone faces fire, gas, electrical, structural, sewage, chemical, or medical danger; if so, keep away and contact the responsible emergency service. Gate two records who controls access. Gate three starts off-site notifications and factual documentation. Gate four authorizes only clearly defined protective work consistent with safety and insurer instructions. Prepare questions about water without attempting to answer them through unsafe entry: Is the source active? Who is qualified to isolate it? Could electricity, structure, sewage, chemicals, or suspect materials be involved? Which floors or units may be affected? What evidence must be preserved? A clean-looking puddle is not enough to determine origin or contamination. Decide in advance how emergency spending will be limited and documented. Set an internal approval threshold appropriate to the organization, require written scope where practicable, and define who can approve necessary exceptions. This internal control does not determine insurance reimbursement. Run a tabletop exercise annually or after management changes. Walk through a hypothetical pipe release or exterior flood, testing contacts, remote access to records, tenant notices, vendor authorization, and insurer notification. Do not simulate physical entry or utility operation. Record weaknesses and assign corrections. Calgary and Alberta guidance should be consulted and date-stamped during updates because municipal recovery and cleaning information may change. Preparedness succeeds when people can make restrained, traceable decisions under pressure.

Questions this guide answers

Should a preparedness plan tell occupants how to shut off utilities?

It should identify locations for qualified responders but must not instruct occupants to touch utilities when conditions are damaged, restricted, or unsafe.

What is the value of a pre-loss contents inventory?

It provides organized ownership, location, serial-number, and purchase evidence without requiring memory after an incident.

What should a tabletop exercise test?

Test contacts, authority, remote records, notifications, privacy, authorization, and documentation—not physical entry or hazardous tasks.

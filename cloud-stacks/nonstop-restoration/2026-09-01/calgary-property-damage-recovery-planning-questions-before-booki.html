Calgary property damage recovery planning: Questions Before Booking

Questions to ask a Calgary recovery provider about access, scope, specialists, records, contents, contracts, changes, and project closeout.

Booking a recovery provider can authorize access, spending, demolition, equipment, contents movement, and future payment obligations. The first call should therefore produce more than reassurance. It should clarify the proposed stage, the provider’s boundaries, required specialists, documentation, contract terms, and the owner’s remaining duties. These questions apply to any candidate and do not assume that one company, delivery model, or service bundle is preferable. Immediate hazards still go to emergency services and responsible authorities before provider selection.

Ask what is being authorized and what remains undecided

What stage are you proposing: emergency protection, investigation, remediation, contents handling, restoration, or reconstruction? Ask for the affected locations, proposed tasks, assumptions, exclusions, quantity basis, equipment basis, and stopping point. If the first authorization must be brief, it should still define rates or charging method, spending controls, documentation, and who can expand the work. Who controls access, and how will restrictions be verified? A provider should acknowledge fire, police, utility, structural, building-management, occupational safety, and specialist controls. Ask who keeps the access log, escorts workers, secures keys, protects alarm information, and communicates with tenants. No booking should require an occupant to enter a prohibited area or touch utilities. Which findings are outside your authority? Listen for clear boundaries around cause, structural adequacy, contamination, mould or health diagnosis, asbestos condition, safe occupancy, code compliance, insurance coverage, and claim settlement. Ask which qualified professional or authority would address each relevant question and whether that party is contracted by the owner, provider, or insurer. Will destructive access occur? If walls, flooring, ceilings, cabinetry, or other assemblies may be opened, ask what pre-disturbance assessment is needed, how suspect hazardous materials are handled, what evidence is captured first, and how the opening is protected. A broad permission to “do whatever is necessary” leaves too much ambiguity.

Ask how people, contents, and evidence will be managed

Who performs the work, and which tasks are subcontracted? Request the names or roles of the project lead, field supervisor, contents coordinator, reconstruction contact, and specialist interfaces. Ask how the provider checks the qualifications required for assigned tasks. Do not infer a credential, licence, or certification that has not been documented and verified through the appropriate source. How will contents be inventoried? The answer should address photographs, item or container identifiers, owner attribution, room of origin, condition notes, packing, transport, storage, access, cleaning decisions, and disposition approval. Clarify charges and what happens to items the owner disputes. Sensitive documents, medication, firearms, valuables, tenant property, and trauma-related material may require distinct legal, privacy, or safety handling. Which project records will be delivered, and when? Ask about daily logs, attendance, photographs, measurements, instruments where relevant, equipment placement and duration, moisture or environmental records, waste documentation, specialist reports, permits, inspections, changes, and invoices. Determine whether the owner receives originals, exports, or portal-only access, and how long access remains available. How is privacy protected? Images can reveal security systems, personal possessions, health information, or incident details. Ask who can view and share records, how insurer delivery occurs, how errors are corrected, and how retention works. Trauma Scene Cleanup and Biohazard Cleanup demand especially careful access, dignity, and information controls.

Ask how money, changes, and completion will work

What documents must be signed before work begins? Read the work authorization, payment schedule, cancellation terms, direction-to-pay language, interest or collection terms, access permissions, liability provisions, and responsibility for amounts not covered by insurance. Ask for time to review anything unclear and obtain legal or insurance advice where appropriate. A provider cannot promise that an insurer will approve its charges. How are estimates structured? Request quantities, units, labour categories, equipment charging, disposal, subcontractors, allowances, taxes, storage, permit fees, and exclusions. Ask whether reconstruction finishes and upgrades are separate. A headline total without these components is difficult to compare. What is the change-order process? It should identify newly found conditions, supporting evidence, response options, cost and schedule effects, and authorized approval. Ask about genuine emergency exceptions and retrospective documentation. Determine who can direct field staff; informal instructions from occupants or tenants can create disputes. What defines completion for each stage? Seek a list of deliverables rather than a general promise: reconciled scope, final photos, readings or specialist evidence where applicable, contents status, permit and inspection results, deficiency list, warranties, and maintenance instructions. Ask how unresolved third-party reports are shown. Who decides re-occupancy? The answer should direct the question to responsible authorities and professionals, not to equipment removal or surface appearance. End by recording the provider’s written answers, unresolved questions, proposal version, references checked, decision-maker, and reason for selection. Booking is appropriate only when the remaining uncertainty is visible and assigned.

Questions this guide answers

What should a limited emergency authorization contain?

State the location, protective tasks, rates or charging basis, spending control, required records, approver, and stopping point.

Should a provider identify subcontractors before booking?

Ask which tasks will be subcontracted, who retains and supervises those parties, and how their scopes and records reach the owner.

What completion evidence should be requested?

Request a scope reconciliation, final images, relevant measurements, contents status, specialist reports, permits, inspections, deficiencies, warranties, and invoices.

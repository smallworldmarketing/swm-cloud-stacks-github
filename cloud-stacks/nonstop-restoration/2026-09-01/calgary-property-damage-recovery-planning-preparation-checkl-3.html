Calgary property damage recovery planning: Preparation Checklist 2

Prepare eight practical records and authority checks that support safer, clearer decisions after a Calgary property-loss incident.

Preparation after property damage means organizing decisions without crossing safety boundaries. This eight-part checklist is for an owner, tenant, or manager who needs a usable control file before substantial work begins. Complete only tasks permitted by the authority controlling access. Never enter a restricted building, touch damaged utilities, disturb suspect materials, or handle sewage, chemicals, sharps, trauma residue, or biological hazards to obtain a better record.

Eight preparation controls before substantial work

1. Record the safety gate. Write down the active restriction, issuing authority, contact route, date, and instructions received. Identify fire, gas, electrical, structural, sewage, chemical, trauma, biohazard, and suspect-material concerns. Do not convert an absence of visible danger into permission to enter. Calgary and Alberta recovery guidance, reviewed September 1, 2026, should be checked for current public instructions relevant to the event. 2. Open an incident chronology. Record discovery time, who was present, notifications, permitted protective actions, and subsequent decisions. Use neutral observations. “Water visible beside the south wall” is more useful than an unsupported statement about cause or liability. Preserve original messages and photographs with their metadata. 3. Create a contact and authority map. List the owner, occupants, property manager, condominium or strata contact where applicable, insurer, emergency authority, contractors, and relevant specialists. Beside each name, state what that party may decide. A restoration provider cannot determine policy coverage, settlement, responsibility, or insurer approval. 4. Build a location-coded photo log. If access is allowed, take an establishing view, room view, and detailed image for each observed condition without touching or moving hazards. Assign stable room and item identifiers. Record the photographer and date. Never remove a barrier or open a wall for documentation. 5. Start the contents register. Give each item or group an identifier, location, brief description, visible condition, photograph reference, and current custody status. Mark sensitive, high-value, tenant-owned, or business-critical property. Require written authority before disposal; do not treat an adjuster conversation or contractor suggestion as automatic consent.

Scope, communication, and closeout preparation

6. Draft a scope-question sheet. Divide the property into structure, envelope, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, finishes, security, contents, and exterior areas. For each, ask what was observed, what remains unknown, who investigates it, what work is proposed, and what evidence will demonstrate completion. Add columns for exclusions, assumptions, permits, inspections, and specialist dependencies. 7. Set communication rules. Choose a primary owner or manager contact and a backup. Decide how urgent notices, ordinary updates, estimate revisions, access requests, contents decisions, and change orders will be delivered. Require written confirmation of material decisions. For multi-unit or commercial properties, separate operational updates from private occupant or employee information. 8. Create the permanent file structure before documents accumulate. Include safety and authority records; insurer communications; photographs; inventories; estimates; authorizations; daily reports; readings; specialist findings; permits; inspection results; invoices; change orders; warranties; and deficiencies. Keep original files, use consistent names, and store a protected backup. A well-indexed record assists future maintenance and claim communication but does not guarantee any insurance outcome. Add two readiness checks. First, define phase gates: allowed access, initial documentation, emergency stabilization, investigation, approved remediation, reconstruction, inspection, deficiencies, and closeout. Name the party who can confirm each gate. Second, decide how unexpected findings will stop dependent work until the right assessment and authorization occur. Preparation is complete when another responsible person can read the file and understand what happened, who controls each decision, what is authorized, and what remains unresolved. It is not complete merely because a contractor has attended or equipment has been placed.

How to use the checklist during an evolving incident

Review the eight controls at every material transition. An access change, newly exposed material, odour, structural movement, additional affected unit, failed temporary protection, or specialist finding can reopen earlier decisions. Update the chronology rather than overwriting it, and preserve superseded scope versions. Hold a short documented coordination check before each new phase. Confirm current access conditions, occupants who require notice, work area boundaries, personal-property controls, responsible supervisor, subcontractors, expected records, and the stop-work route. If a permit or inspection question exists, consult the City of Calgary’s current process or an appropriate professional. The City’s homeowner inspection information was reviewed September 1, 2026; requirements must be verified for the specific project. Do not turn the checklist into a do-it-yourself cleanup instruction. It deliberately avoids technical targets, sampling protocols, hazardous-material handling, utility manipulation, and declarations of clearance or occupancy. Those judgments depend on the incident and qualified parties. When comparing providers, give each candidate the same scope-question sheet. Ask them to identify missing information instead of rewarding certainty unsupported by evidence. Request readable sample records and a written explanation of handoffs. Response-time promises, broad insurance assurances, or an undifferentiated total should not replace this review. At closeout, reconcile the file against the preparation index. Obtain final scope revisions, authorized changes, completion photographs, equipment and measurement records where applicable, contents custody and disposition records, specialist reports, inspection outcomes, warranties, care information, invoices, and open-deficiency dates. Keep facts, professional opinions, insurer decisions, and owner approvals distinguishable. That separation makes the record more reliable long after the immediate event has passed.

Questions this guide answers

Should photographs be taken inside a restricted area?

No. Documentation never overrides an access restriction or unresolved hazard.

What belongs in a permanent property-loss file?

Keep authority records, communications, photos, inventories, scopes, authorizations, logs, findings, permits, inspections, changes, invoices, warranties, and deficiencies.

Why preserve old scope versions?

They show what was known and authorized at each stage and help explain later changes.

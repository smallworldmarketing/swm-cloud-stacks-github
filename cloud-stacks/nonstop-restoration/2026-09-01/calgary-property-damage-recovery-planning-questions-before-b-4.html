Calgary property damage recovery planning: Questions Before Booking 3

Nine booking questions that clarify access, scope, specialist boundaries, records, contracts, contents, and closeout before property work starts.

Booking a site visit or property-protection task should create a defined next step, not an open-ended permission to perform whatever appears necessary. Before confirming, establish whether access is allowed, which hazards or specialists control the sequence, what the appointment includes, and what document will follow. Do not ask a provider to enter a restricted building, touch affected utilities, or disturb suspect material. Emergency authorities and qualified professionals retain their respective safety roles, while the insurer decides policy matters. These nine questions help Calgary property owners, tenants, and managers evaluate a proposed engagement without relying on ratings, response-time claims, or invented credentials.

Nine questions to ask the provider

1. What is the exact purpose of the appointment? Distinguish a non-invasive observation, emergency board-up, water-removal proposal, contents review, specialist assessment, remediation estimate, or reconstruction discussion. Ask what is expressly outside the visit. A booking for inspection should not become implied authorization for demolition. 2. What access information is required? Provide restrictions, escorts, keys or security procedures, occupant notices, privacy limits, and the name of the person controlling entry. Ask how the provider responds if site conditions differ from the briefing. Ownership alone does not override an authority’s restriction. 3. Which stop-work conditions does your team recognize? The answer should address escalation for fire, gas, electrical, structural, sewage, chemical, trauma, biohazard, and suspect-material concerns without promising that one contractor resolves all of them. Ask who communicates a stop and where it is recorded. 4. Which service boundary applies? Have the provider separate emergency mitigation, water removal, flood cleanup, mould work, smoke-related cleaning, sewage cleanup, contents handling, asbestos abatement, temporary security, structural repair, and rebuilding. Not every event requires every service, and similarly named activities may have different inclusions. 5. What work is direct, subcontracted, or referred? Request the legal contracting party and the role of each participant. Ask who supervises, who holds specialist records, who communicates changes, and whether separate customer agreements are needed. Do not infer qualifications; request the evidence relevant to the proposed task and verify it through appropriate sources. 6. What will the written scope contain? Look for areas, activities, quantities, materials, labour, equipment assumptions, protections, exclusions, dependencies, customer duties, evidence, and change procedure. Ask whether alternatives will be shown separately. Avoid agreeing to a vague promise to restore everything. 7. How are contents controlled? Determine who inventories, photographs, packs, transports, cleans, stores, authorizes disposal, and records return. Ask how pre-existing condition, ownership, privacy, and chain of custody are handled. Nobody should move unsafe material solely to complete an estimate. 8. Which records are delivered, and when? Discuss pre-work images, daily logs, measurements where applicable, equipment placement, subcontractor records, waste or disposition documents, specialist reports, permits, inspection outcomes, warranties, invoices, and closeout notes. Define file format, naming, retention, and access. 9. What must the customer read before confirming? Request work authorization, payment and deposit terms, direction-to-pay language, cancellation rules, access permission, photography consent, subcontracting terms, dispute provisions, and change-order controls. Seek legal advice for unclear effects. Insurer involvement does not automatically remove the customer’s contractual obligations.

Evaluate the answers before placing the booking

Put responses into an evidence table with columns for question, provider answer, supporting document, unresolved point, decision owner, and deadline. An answer is stronger when it identifies a boundary and record. “We handle it” is weaker than a description of the named task, responsible role, prerequisite, exclusion, and deliverable. Check the proposed sequence. If disturbance may occur, has the suspect-asbestos question been addressed? If sewage, trauma, or biohazard conditions are reported, are privacy, restricted access, worker-safety, and waste responsibilities explicit? If rebuilding is discussed, who discovers Calgary permit and inspection requirements? If re-occupancy is mentioned, has the provider correctly left that decision with the responsible authorities and professionals? Separate three authorizations: permission to attend, permission to inspect within stated limits, and permission to perform work. State the approved areas and expiry or review point. If an immediate property-protection measure is authorized, keep its boundary distinct from later remediation or reconstruction. This permits urgent, properly directed action without forcing a premature commitment to every phase. Send a written confirmation showing property address, appointment purpose, access contact, restrictions, attendees, permitted activities, prohibited activities, requested deliverable, and billing basis. Correct misunderstandings before arrival. Store the confirmation with the incident chronology. After the appointment, request the promised record and compare it with the agreed purpose. Log any new observation without adopting an unsupported cause. If the provider proposes added work, apply the ordinary scope, safety, insurance, and authorization review rather than treating the visit as continuing consent. The booking succeeds when it produces a clear decision package, even if the responsible choice is to pause for an authority, insurer, specialist, legal adviser, or different provider.

Questions this guide answers

Does booking a site visit authorize the provider to begin work?

Not automatically. Permission to attend, inspect, and perform work should be granted separately with defined areas and limits.

What should a booking confirmation identify?

It should identify the property, purpose, access contact, restrictions, attendees, permitted and prohibited activities, expected deliverable, and billing basis.

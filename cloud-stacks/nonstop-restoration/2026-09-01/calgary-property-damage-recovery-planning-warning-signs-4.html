Calgary property damage recovery planning: Warning Signs 3

Use red, amber, and record-review signals to decide when to stop, escalate, clarify authority, or withhold approval during recovery.

A warning sign should trigger a defined response, not a diagnosis. Some observations require immediate distance and contact with emergency authorities; others mean work should pause until a qualified person resolves an uncertainty. Contract and record problems may justify withholding authorization without implying that a provider acted improperly. This framework separates those decisions. It does not teach readers to inspect hazardous areas. Do not enter a restricted building, approach instability, handle sewage or biohazards, operate affected utilities, or disturb material that could contain asbestos. Observe only from locations and circumstances already confirmed as accessible, and pass safety judgments to the responsible authority or professional.

Red signals: withdraw, restrict, and escalate

Treat fire, smoke suggesting an active event, a gas odour, sparking or exposed electrical components, moving or visibly unstable building elements, chemical release, uncontrolled sewage, sharps, trauma residue, and unknown biological material as stop signals. Leave or remain outside as appropriate and contact emergency services, the utility, property authority, or qualified specialist. Do not rely on a restoration appointment as the response to an immediate threat. A posted restriction, barrier, verbal prohibition from an authority, or locked area under controlled access is also red. Neither ownership nor concern for belongings overrides it. Record the instruction from a safe place and notify affected decision-makers. Do not ask a contractor to bypass the restriction for photographs or a quote. Suspect asbestos is a disturbance stop. Age alone does not prove that a material contains asbestos, and appearance cannot reliably settle the question. Pause cutting, drilling, sanding, demolition, or moving debris until the appropriate assessment and work requirements are established. Alberta publishes asbestos worker-training requirements, while workplace obligations may also arise under occupational health and safety law. Training status does not itself provide a result for a particular material. Possible mould growth, unusual odour, or a history of wetting calls for moisture-source and extent questions, not a health diagnosis. Alberta Health Services offers public-health information about mould. People with health concerns should seek medical guidance; a property contractor should not diagnose symptoms. Air testing should not be treated as a universal pass-or-fail answer because the decision may also depend on sources, materials, extent, work scope, visual findings, moisture information, and specialist judgment.

Amber signals: pause authorization and demand clarity

An estimate that omits the property area, quantities, exclusions, assumptions, or change process is an amber commercial signal. Ask for a revision before comparing totals. Broad phrases such as “complete restoration” or “insurance-approved” should be unpacked into actual activities and evidence. A contractor cannot decide coverage or insurer approval. Unclear access authority is another pause point. Confirm who authorized entry, photography, contents movement, destructive investigation, and work. Multi-unit properties may require separate decisions for units, common areas, and neighbouring spaces. Privacy-sensitive events need controls over names, images, records, and waste information. Watch for technical conclusions without supporting role or record: a claim that a property is safe, dry, clear, code-compliant, or ready for occupancy should identify who has authority or professional responsibility, the inspected boundary, method, date, limitations, and underlying report. One phase’s completion cannot automatically answer every other discipline’s question. A request to sign immediately deserves separation into components. An authorized temporary measure may be time-sensitive while a broad reconstruction agreement is not. Read work authorization, payment obligations, direction-to-pay wording, cancellation provisions, dispute terms, and permissions for subcontracting or photography. Obtain legal advice if their effect is uncertain. Finally, repeated undocumented verbal changes signal record failure. Send a factual recap identifying the instruction, time, person, cost or schedule effect, and whether work is paused. Require the designated decision-maker to confirm. If conditions genuinely require immediate action, record the authority and safety rationale as soon as possible.

Green does not mean risk-free

A green status means a named prerequisite is documented, not that the whole property is safe. Examples include written access permission for a defined area, an accepted scope version, an approved change order, a received specialist report, or a completed municipal inspection for specified work. Label each item with its boundary and expiry or review condition. Maintain a signal log containing observation, source, category, immediate response, responsible person, requested evidence, and resolution. Attach photographs only when obtained lawfully and safely. Never change red to green merely because time passed or equipment stopped running. Before closing a signal, ask whether another discipline still owns part of the decision. A fire authority’s release may not resolve structural, electrical, environmental, or permit issues. A remediation record may not authorize reconstruction. A contractor completion note may not decide re-occupancy. This disciplined boundary is the core of safety-first planning: act quickly on genuine danger, slow down unsupported commitments, and preserve enough evidence for accountable decisions.

Questions this guide answers

Does visible mould establish a health diagnosis?

No. Property observations can prompt moisture and professional-assessment questions, while personal health concerns belong with medical professionals.

Does a green record for one task mean the building is safe to occupy?

No. It confirms only the stated prerequisite or work boundary; responsible authorities and professionals handle re-occupancy decisions.
